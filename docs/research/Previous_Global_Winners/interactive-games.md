## 1. Egyptian Global Winners

### Zumorroda-X (2025 Global Winner)

- **Recognition:** Art & Technology Global Award.
- **Challenge:** NASA Farm Navigators: Using NASA Data Exploration in
  Agriculture.
- **Location:** Egypt.
- **Format:** Collection of mini-games.
- **What they built:** Players take the role of a farmer travelling
  around the world. Through mini-games, they encounter agricultural
  challenges such as heat waves and flooding and learn how farmers
  adapt to environmental conditions.
- **Why it matters:** It turns a broad science topic into smaller
  playable activities. The player learns through action rather than
  only reading an explanation.
- **Design lessons:** Give the player a clear role; break a broad
  subject into distinct situations; make the challenge itself
  communicate the science; use variety without losing the central
  theme.
- **Official source:** [NASA's 2025 Global Winners
  announcement](https://www.nasa.gov/learning-resources/stem-engagement-at-nasa/nasa-announces-2025-international-space-apps-challenge-global-winners/).
- **Public resources:** [NASA Space Apps team
  page](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2025/find-a-team/zumorroda-x/)
  (linked from NASA's announcement; the contributor opened it in her
  browser on 2026-10-31 and it lists the game website and the demo video
  below).
- **Game website:** [henrysfarmadventures.vercel.app](https://henrysfarmadventures.vercel.app/)
  (listed on the team page; the contributor opened and played it on
  2026-10-31; the assistant confirmed on 2026-11-05 that the site loads)
- **Demo video:** [Google Drive file "nasa

### TerraTales (2024 Global Winner)

- **Recognition:** Best Storytelling Global Award.
- **Challenge:** Tell Us a Climate Story!
- **Location:** Cairo, Egypt.
- **Format:** Story-driven website with an interactive game and
  climate-forecasting component.
- **What they built:** Mother Earth meets three children—"South,"
  "North," and "Neutral"—to discuss climate changes in different
  regions. Brazil, Germany, and Egypt are representative examples. The
  website draws on NASA Greenhouse Gas Center data. It includes an
  interactive game intended to show how individual choices affect the
  environment, plus a section forecasting possible future outcomes if
  no action is taken.
- **Why it matters:** It connects characters and regional stories with
  an interactive activity and future-facing consequences.
- **Technology reported in the initial research notes:** HTML, CSS,
  JavaScript, Python, TensorFlow, Keras, Canva, and Leonardo AI.
  NASA's public project summary confirms the website, NASA data, game,
  and forecasting component; the stack is recorded here as reported in
  the research notes, not independently itemized in the NASA summary.
- **Design lessons:** Use characters to make abstract subjects easier
  to follow; give the player a reason to care; connect action to
  outcome; combine narrative and evidence without turning the
  experience into a lecture.
- **Public resources:** [NASA Space Apps project
  profile](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/nasa-space-apps-2024/find-a-team/terratales/?tab=project)
  · [TerraTales website](https://terratalesjourney.netlify.app/) ·
  [Project demonstration
  video](https://drive.google.com/file/d/18fZCTpWv1b-nGf7o6hufBO35blM6vzQx/view).
- **Source repository:** Not found in the reviewed sources.

### LunarTech Ensemble (2023 Global Winner)

- **Recognition:** Best Use of Science Global Award.
- **Challenge:** Make a Moonquake Map 2.0!
- **Location:** Egypt.
- **Format:** Website and immersive game.
- **What they built:** A website and immersive game to help users
  understand and visualize lunar seismic data gathered by instruments
  left behind during NASA's Apollo missions.
- **Why it matters:** This is one of the closest matches in this set
  to a space-science exploration game. It makes real lunar science the
  subject of an interactive experience.
- **Design lessons:** Let exploration reveal science; translate
  unfamiliar measurements into understandable interactions; make the
  scientific subject central to the experience rather than background
  decoration.
- **Public resources:** [NASA's 2023 Global Winners
  announcement](https://science.nasa.gov/directorates/smd/2023-nasa-international-space-apps-challenge-announces-10-global-winners/)
  · [NASA Space Apps team/project
  profile](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2023/find-a-team/lunartech-ensemble/?tab=project).
- **Playable demo / source repository:** Not verified in the reviewed
  sources.

## 2. International Global Winners

### Eco-Metropolis (2024 Global Winner)

- **Team:** Innovisionaries.
- **Recognition:** Most Inspirational Global Award.
- **Challenge:** SDGs in the Classroom.
- **Location:** Sharjah, United Arab Emirates.
- **Format:** City-building simulation game.
- **What they built:** A city-building game in which players make
  urban-planning and resource-management decisions informed by
  real-world environmental data.
- **Why it matters:** Its central mechanic is decision-making. The
  player's choices shape the simulated city and its sustainability.
- **Design lessons:** Make decisions change the game world; show
  consequences clearly; let players experiment with different
  solutions.
- **Public resources:** [NASA's 2024 Global Winners

### Spacebee (2023 Global Winner)

- **Recognition:** Best Use of Technology Global Award.
- **Challenge:** Make a Moonquake Map 2.0!
- **Location:** United States and Argentina (Universal Event).
- **Format:** Website presenting lunar seismic data.
- **What they built:** A website that integrates moonquake data
  collected by seismometers deployed during NASA's Apollo missions.
  It helps users explore moonquake locations, types, and dates, as well
  as data from the Apollo Lunar Surface Experiments Package (ALSEP).
- **Why it matters:** It demonstrates how scientific datasets can be
  organized into an accessible interactive experience.
- **Design lessons:** Make complex scientific information easier to
  explore; organize data around questions users may want to investigate;
  use visual presentation to make scientific information understandable.
- **Official source:** [NASA's 2023 Global Winners
  announcement](https://science.nasa.gov/directorates/smd/2023-nasa-international-space-apps-challenge-announces-10-global-winners/).
- **Playable demo / source repository:** Not verified in the reviewed
  sources.

### Space Quest Maidens (2023 Global Winner)

- **Recognition:** Most Inspirational Global Award.
- **Challenge:** Eclipses: Perspective is Everything.
- **Location:** Brazil.
- **Format:** Interactive educational tool.
- **What they built:** **ECLIPSE: CELESTIAL SHADOWS**, an interactive
  educational tool designed to teach children about the mechanics of
  eclipses.
- **Why it matters:** It demonstrates how an astronomical phenomenon
  can be explained through interaction rather than text alone.
- **Design lessons:** Choose interactions that make scientific
  relationships visible; design for the intended audience; use
  interaction to clarify a concept rather than decorate a page.
- **Official source:** [NASA's 2023 Global Winners
  announcement](https://science.nasa.gov/directorates/smd/2023-nasa-international-space-apps-challenge-announces-10-global-winners/).

## 5. Patterns Worth Learning

### A. Make science affect gameplay

A weak approach is to show a NASA fact panel unrelated to the player's
actions. A stronger approach makes the scientific concept determine what
the player investigates, solves, builds, or decides.

**References:** LunarTech Ensemble, Zumorroda-X, EcoRangers, EcoNet.

### B. Give the player a reason to care

A story, role, goal, or consequence makes the scientific subject
meaningful.

**References:** TerraTales and Team Voyagers.

### C. Make choices produce understandable consequences

Players should connect an action with an outcome in the game world.

**Reference:** Eco-Metropolis.

### D. Use interaction deliberately

Exploration, puzzles, simulation, decision-making, and mini-games are
different tools. Choose a mechanic because it helps communicate the
concept or advance the story.

**References:** LunarTech Ensemble, Spacebee, Space Quest Maidens,
Zumorroda-X.

### E. Teach Through Play

The player should learn what is needed to progress from the experience
itself. Explanations can support the game, but should not replace
meaningful interaction.

## 6. Application to the Team's Starting Game Concept

The team's starting idea, as described by Rana: a character wakes up
somewhere unfamiliar and the player gradually discovers the situation
through exploration and clues. This is a working idea. The team's
selected challenge is Space Mission Design Game (see the [2026-11-01
meeting notes](../../team/meeting-notes/2026-11-01-challenge-decision-and-team-setup.md)),
and the team's proposed pitch concept is in
[pitch/odyssey-of-meat-a-roid/README.md](../../../pitch/odyssey-of-meat-a-roid/README.md),
where the character wakes up on the wrong planet and the player plans a
mission to get him home. The game design itself has not been formally
decided.

These are design directions inspired by the case studies, not claims
about what those teams built:

1. **Mystery:** Start with a clear unanswered question. Do not explain
   the entire backstory immediately.
2. **Exploration:** Let the player inspect rooms, objects, maps, logs,
   and environmental clues.
3. **Scientific puzzles:** Make selected puzzles depend on real
   planetary or space-mission conditions. Verify the science before
   turning it into a puzzle.
4. **Decisions:** Let choices affect resources, routes, mission status,
   or later events.
5. **Narrative revelation:** Reveal why the character is there through
   evidence the player discovers.
6. **Learning by doing:** Introduce scientific ideas when the player
   needs them to solve a problem.

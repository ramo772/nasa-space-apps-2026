# Audio and comedy identity

**Status:** Canonical identity v1

**Scope:** Dialogue rhythm, vocal reactions, interface audio, workshop sound, music and accessibility.

## Principle

The tone is **comic scientific problem-solving**. Humor makes a consequence memorable; it must not replace the explanation.

All dialogue, performances, music, and sound effects must be original or appropriately licensed. Never sample or recreate a commercial film's recorded audio.

## Comedy engine

Each comic beat should follow this pattern:

1. **Expectation:** Nadir confidently predicts a result.
2. **Contradiction:** A small visible system consequence disagrees.
3. **Recognition:** Silent pose or eye-line toward RIVO.
4. **Clarification:** RIVO explains the actual relationship.
5. **Agency:** The player receives a clear next choice.

Use comedy at scene openings, between decisions, and after results. Do not interrupt the player while reading a metric or source.

## Approved joke families

- System literalism: RIVO follows Nadir's vague request precisely.
- Prop callback: the wrench or coffee bulb reappears at the wrong moment.
- Rule of three: two expected responses followed by one small technical surprise.
- Scale mismatch: Nadir prepares a dramatic solution for a modest problem.
- Premature celebration: Nadir celebrates one green meter before noticing another constraint.
- Technical translation: RIVO converts jargon into a plain comparison.

## Original vocal reactions

Nadir may use short nonverbal reactions: inhale, clipped two-note groan, confused hum, relieved exhale, and suppressed laugh. Record these from the selected adult performer as new material.

Do not direct the performer to imitate a specific actor, quote a recognizable reaction, or match the pitch or timing of a commercial recording.

RIVO uses short original notification tones paired with captions or visual states:

- one soft cyan rising interval: connection found;
- one low coral pulse: constraint warning;
- two even cream clicks: source available;
- a small descending chirp: Nadir's prediction did not survive validation.

Exact notes and samples will be defined only after the audio creator and license are recorded.

## Workshop sound palette

- magnetic clacks;
- soft relay ticks;
- restrained servo movement;
- air-circulation bed;
- fabric and harness movement;
- low hover tone for RIVO;
- quiet interface confirmation tones;
- no constant alarm loop.

The workshop must feel inhabited and mechanical, not magical.

## Music direction

Use an original combination of light hand percussion, restrained pizzicato gestures, modular synth pulses, and warm bass. Music supports curiosity and timing without copying a film score.

- Workshop: curious, steady, low density.
- Wrong-orbit reveal: brief silence, then one asymmetric motif.
- Mission building: pulse follows decision cadence without implying urgency.
- Feasible result: resolve the motif without a heroic military fanfare.
- Infeasible result: remain encouraging; do not use failure stingers that shame the player.

## Dialogue rules

- One joke before or after an explanation, never inside the critical fact.
- RIVO uses at most two sentences at a time.
- Nadir may misunderstand a system, but the UI must not.
- Avoid ridicule of beginners, accents, cultures, scientific ignorance, or failed choices.
- Subtitles preserve the meaning of nonverbal reactions, for example `[uncertain groan]`.

Example original exchange:

> **Nadir:** RIVO, tell me the transfer was efficient.
>
> **RIVO:** It was extremely efficient.
>
> **Nadir:** I hear a “but.”
>
> **RIVO:** It was efficient at going somewhere else.

## Accessibility

- Caption dialogue and meaningful sound effects.
- Provide independent music, voice, and effects volume controls.
- Never require audio to understand a choice or warning.
- Pair RIVO tones with a visible face state, icon, and text.
- Avoid sudden extreme volume changes.
- Support replaying story dialogue without replaying the full scene.

## Required provenance

For every audio asset, record creator, creation method, source, license, date, edits, and intended use. If a real performer is identifiable, keep written consent appropriate for the NASA submission and media terms.

# Challenge Selection — How to Choose the Right Hackathon Challenge

**Status:** Draft  
**Type:** Workshop Notes  
**Created:** 2026-11-03  
**Last reviewed:** 2026-11-05  
**Session type:** Live Session  
**Session topic:** A Practical Framework for Evaluating and Selecting Hackathon Challenges.

## Purpose

Document the key insights and practical methodology shared during the live session about selecting the most suitable challenge in a hackathon.

The session focuses on evaluating challenges through four main factors: Impact, Feasibility, Time, and New Skills. It also highlights the importance of resource availability, user-centered thinking, planning, and documentation before starting implementation.

## Content

### 1. Understanding the Challenge Before Choosing

The speaker introduced a methodology that involves reading the challenge three times, with a different purpose for each reading:

1. **First Reading — Surface Reading:** Get a general understanding of the challenge and identify its main idea.
2. **Second Reading — Deep Reading:** Understand the problem, requirements, and expected outcomes in more detail.
3. **Third Reading — Analytical Reading:** Evaluate the challenge carefully and answer four key questions to determine whether it is suitable for the team.

This approach helps teams avoid choosing challenges based only on their initial impression.

### 2. The Four Challenge Evaluation Factors

Each challenge should be evaluated using four main factors:

**1. Impact**

- Evaluate the potential impact of solving the problem.
- Consider how the proposed solution could benefit users or address a real-world issue.

**2. Feasibility**

- Determine whether the challenge can realistically be solved.
- Check whether the required resources, datasets, and technical capabilities are available.
- Consider the team's ability to implement the proposed solution.

**3. Time**

- Evaluate whether the challenge can be completed within the available time.
- Consider the actual hackathon duration, such as 48 hours.
- Avoid choosing challenges that require more time than the team has available.

**4. New Skills**

- Consider the new skills and knowledge the team could gain.
- Evaluate whether the challenge provides an opportunity to learn new technologies or explore unfamiliar areas.

### 3. Challenge Scoring Method

The session introduced a simple scoring system to compare different challenges.

- Evaluate each of the four factors on a scale from 1 to 5.
- Calculate the total score for each challenge.
- Compare the results and select the challenge with the highest overall score.

The purpose of this method is to make challenge selection more structured and objective.

### 4. Check Resources and Datasets

Before committing to a challenge, teams should verify that the necessary resources are available.

Important resources include:

- **Datasets:** Data provided by organizations such as NASA and CSA.
- **Software Tools:** Technologies and tools required to build the solution.
- **Scientific Research:** Papers, studies, and existing research related to the challenge.

A lack of available resources or datasets is a common reason why teams fail to complete their projects.

Therefore, resource availability should be checked during the challenge selection process, not after implementation begins.

### 5. Choose a Realistic Challenge

- Do not automatically choose the most difficult or complex challenge.
- Select a challenge that can realistically be addressed within the available time.
- Consider the team's current capabilities and available resources.
- Prioritize completing a meaningful solution over attempting something too ambitious.

For example, in a 48-hour hackathon, the team should focus on a problem that can be addressed within those 48 hours.

### 6. Create a Clear Proposal

Before starting implementation, teams should prepare a clear Proposal that defines the project direction.

The Proposal should include:

1. **Problem:** What problem are you trying to solve?
2. **Solution:** What is your proposed approach?
3. **Methodology:** How will you develop and implement the solution?
4. **Expected Results:** What outcomes do you expect from the project?

A clear Proposal helps the team understand the project scope and maintain a shared direction.

### 7. The Importance of Documentation

- Document every step throughout the project.
- Keep track of decisions, progress, and implementation details.
- Documentation makes it easier to review previous work.
- It also supports future improvements and helps the team understand the development process.

Documentation should be part of the workflow from the beginning, not something added only at the end.

### 8. Focus on the User

- The user should be at the center of the solution.
- Do not focus only on the technical implementation.
- Understand the actual needs and problems of the intended users.
- Evaluate the proposed solution based on the value it provides to them.

A technically advanced solution is not necessarily a successful one if it does not address the user's real needs.

### 9. Bootcamp Participation and Credits

The session also highlighted an opportunity related to training participation:

- Teams that attend Bootcamp training sessions receive higher priority when accessing limited Credits.
- Credits are limited resources, so participation in the training sessions can provide an advantage.

## Key Takeaways

1. Read every challenge three times: Surface Reading, Deep Reading, and Analytical Reading.
2. Evaluate challenges using four factors: **Impact, Feasibility, Time, and New Skills**.
3. Score each factor from 1 to 5 and compare the total scores.
4. Verify the availability of datasets, software tools, and scientific research before choosing a challenge.
5. Choose a realistic challenge that can be addressed within the available time.
6. Prepare a clear Proposal covering the problem, solution, methodology, and expected results.
7. Document every step to support review and future improvements.
8. Keep the user at the center of the solution rather than focusing only on technical complexity.
9. Attend Bootcamp sessions to gain priority when accessing limited Credits.

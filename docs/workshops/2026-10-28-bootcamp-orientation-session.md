## Purpose

Document the key information and guidance shared during the NASA Space Apps Challenge Bootcamp Orientation Session for the Cairo local event.

The session introduces participants to the NASA Space Apps Challenge, explains the hackathon journey, outlines the bootcamp structure, and provides practical advice on team formation, challenge selection, and preparation.

A key message is that participation is open to people from different backgrounds, and having a technical background is not a requirement.

## Content

### What is NASA Space Apps Challenge?

- NASA Space Apps Challenge is a global hackathon where participants work on real-world challenges using NASA's publicly available data.
- Participants collaborate in teams to develop creative solutions within 48 hours.
- The challenge encourages innovation, collaboration, and problem-solving across different disciplines.

### Cairo Local Event

- The Cairo local event is organized by **IEEE Young Professionals**.
- The Cairo event has been running since 2015.
- Cairo teams achieved global recognition in previous editions, according to the speaker's claims:
  - **2024:** TerraTales (Cairo).
  - **2025:** Zumorroda-X (Egypt).
- The local hackathon provides participants with an opportunity to collaborate, develop solutions, and compete as part of the global challenge.

### Bootcamp Information

- The bootcamp will take place at **Nile University**.
- Bootcamp registration is separate from global NASA Space Apps registration.
- Registering for the global event does not automatically register participants for the Cairo local event.
- Participants should register through the bootcamp registration QR code before the deadline.
- Some technical workshops may require participants to bring a laptop.

### Hackathon and Bootcamp Dates

The session and event dates are:

- **Orientation session:** 2026-10-28.
- **Bootcamp (Nile University):** Friday, 2026-10-30.
- **Cairo local hackathon:** 2026-11-13 to 2026-11-14.

### Team Formation and Diversity

Building a team with different skills and backgrounds is highly encouraged.

Teams should combine members with different areas of expertise, such as:

- **Research:** Understanding the problem and gathering relevant information.
- **Data Analysis:** Exploring and interpreting available datasets.
- **Technology:** Developing and implementing technical solutions.
- **Design:** Creating user-friendly interfaces and visual experiences.
- **Storytelling:** Communicating the project idea and its impact.
- **Art:** Contributing creative and visual elements.

The main recommendation is to avoid forming teams where everyone has exactly the same skill set.

### Bootcamp Structure

The bootcamp will include different types of sessions:

1. **Understanding the Journey:** Introducing the hackathon process and helping participants understand what to expect.
2. **Technical Workshops:** Providing practical knowledge and workshops covering specific technical topics.
3. **Team Formation:** Giving participants an opportunity to connect with others and build diverse teams.

Participants can attend even if they do not already have a team or a project idea.

### Preparing for the Challenges

Before attending the bootcamp, participants are encouraged to:

- Visit the official NASA Space Apps global website.
- Read the available challenge briefs carefully.
- Identify the challenges that interest them most.
- Think about whether they can develop a solution for a selected challenge.
- Prepare five questions to help analyze the challenge and evaluate the possibility of creating a solution.

### Using AI Tools

- AI tools are allowed during the hackathon.
- Participants must disclose which AI tools they used in developing their solutions.
- Transparency about AI usage is an important part of the project process.

### Communication and Announcements

Participants should follow the event's social media platforms.

Important announcements may include:

- Bootcamp agenda.
- Venue details.
- Registration updates.
- Corrections and other event-related information.

## Key Takeaways

1. NASA Space Apps is a 48-hour global hackathon that uses NASA's public data to solve real-world challenges.
2. You do not need a technical background to participate.
3. Diverse teams with complementary skills are more valuable than teams with identical backgrounds.
4. Bootcamp registration and Cairo local hackathon registration are separate processes.
5. Read the challenge briefs before attending the bootcamp.
6. Prepare five questions to evaluate whether a challenge can be turned into a practical solution.
7. AI tools are allowed, but their usage must be disclosed.
8. Team formation is an important part of the bootcamp, and attending without a team or idea is completely acceptable.
9. The main goal is learning, collaboration, and gaining experience rather than focusing only on winning.
10. Follow official social media channels to stay updated on event announcements.

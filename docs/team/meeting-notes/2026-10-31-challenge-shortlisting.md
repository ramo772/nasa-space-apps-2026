## New ideas (triage; not approved features)
**Team ideas — none of this is from the official challenge text.** The "official text" remarks below are the note-taker's reading of the official pages and were not checked independently (the official site blocked automated access on 2026-11-03).

### Storytelling approach (shared by Abduallah Waleed, ACT-01)
- Stories are built from character, goal, conflict, obstacles and resolution (04:01).
- Any project can be reduced to one sentence of problem and solution, which helps shape its story (07:03).
- Science storytelling can blend data with a narrative, for example a character who guides others through a problem (12:04).
- A game can also be framed as a story: the player lives a story while playing, and software can be presented in a story format (22:17).

### Space Mission Design Game (finalist)
- **Team idea:** the player leads a crew with a limited budget and time to reach a destination; resources chosen at the start must last the whole trip; the player picks one of three stories — searching for signs of life on a moon, searching for water and life under an icy surface, and an ice planet such as Neptune (58:07; the transcript is cut off after this point).
- **Official text (per note-taker):** the player sets the mission goal; these specific stories are not prescribed. Secondary news coverage describes it as a game in which users plan and run their own mission.

### Interplanetary Survival Guide: Martian Map (finalist)
- No design ideas for this challenge are recorded in the available notes. It also appears on the note-taker's list of GIS-heavy challenges — see "Blockers / open questions".

### Abandoned but not Forgotten (discussed as an idea; not a finalist)
- **Team ideas:** framing the discarded equipment as sent before animals or humans (the team's wording); an interactive solar system map where each planet shows a symbol for the devices sent or lost there, and selecting a device shows a short illustrated story (26:49); choose the age group first (7 to 12 or 12 to 16) or ask the user's age and adapt the output, possibly through AI prompts; define narrative perspective and audience before designing the output (29:54); accessibility through clear language and text alternatives, such as a description of the story (29:26).
- **Official text (per note-taker):** it encourages clear communication; the details above are the team's.
- Not the meeting's choice. The tl;dv AI summary's claim that this was the tentative choice is disputed by the note-taker (see above).

### Other challenges discussed
- **Earth System Trend Detective:** a tool that detects changes in a chosen region (temperature, humidity, rainfall) with simple explanations of each factor and an interactive visualisation (34:36 and 35:53). Set aside (DEC-03).
- **Health monitoring:** organs send the astronaut a notification when a risk appears (for example high radiation) and suggest what to do (44:03). A concern was raised that this is hard to make creative. The notification idea is the team's addition; official text per note-taker is entering health data, evaluating it and giving advice.
- **Flame in Freefall:** a dashboard that explains microgravity flame experiments and what each added to the one before; the strong comparison between experiments is the team's emphasis. Difficulty noted: finding suitable experiments to compare. Official text (per note-taker): a dashboard that summarises, orders and explains results.
- **Microgravity and physical-science informatics:** the challenge concerns physical and biological sciences, and a 2025 global award project connected microgravity research to informatics; the group said it must stay with the challenge's own data.
- **Lunar landing:** a visualisation tool to choose the best landing position from sunlight and communications. No outcome recorded beyond the GIS set-aside below.
- **Game-style ideas:** the game-design workshop slides (recorded in `docs/workshops/2026-10-30-game-simulation-design.md`) showed games where indicators drive outcomes and only one decision is correct. A concern was raised that an idea like this had already been built by many teams; the transcript is unclear about which idea.

## Reasons challenges were set aside (reported by the note-taker)
- GIS-dependent challenges were set aside because nobody on the team has GIS skills. The note-taker identified these as the most GIS-heavy, from her reading of the official pages (not independently verified):
  1. Interplanetary Survival Guide: Martian Map — an interactive, multi-layer map of the Martian surface.
  2. Identify Earth Locations that Analog the Permanent Moon Base Locations and Mars — finding places on Earth that resemble lunar or Martian sites and showing them on a map (the official text mentions GIS, per the note-taker).
  3. Lunar Landing Site Navigator — comparing lunar landing sites and visualising the positions of the Sun and Earth.
  4. Harmonization of MODIS and VIIRS Hot Spots — a map or calendar of fire activity over time and regions.
  5. Dancing with the SARs — tracking changes on Earth's surface with radar data.
- **Be An Earth System Trend Detective!** was set aside after Engy Refaai proposed removing it: the team does not want a project about the environment, trees, fires or earthquakes (DEC-03).
- Advice relayed second-hand by Shahnda Elmasry from a mentor: follow the written challenge description closely; if it asks for a game or a website, deliver that format; judges focus on following the description and on using NASA data. Not all 14 challenges suit this team.

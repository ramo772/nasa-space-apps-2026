# Global award fit strategy

**Status:** Planning lens; re-verify official 2026 award definitions at kickoff

**Date:** 2026-11-07

NASA Space Apps has historically recognized ten global award categories. The user-provided list aligns with the official 2023 categories and the 2025 winner announcement, but a current 2026 award-definition page was not located as of this review.

Official references:

- <https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2023/awards/>
- <https://www.nasa.gov/learning-resources/stem-engagement-at-nasa/nasa-announces-2025-international-space-apps-challenge-global-winners/>

Do not attempt to optimize equally for all categories. That would create an unfocused submission. The project should be excellent in two categories and credible in several others.

## Fit matrix

| Award | Current fit | How the game fits | Evidence required before claiming strength |
|---|---|---|---|
| Best Use of Science | High potential | Deterministic mission trade-offs, ISAM framing, transparent simplification and causal review | Sourced equations/thresholds, units, assumptions, rule tests and review by someone technically competent |
| Best Use of Data | Medium / blocked | NASA data or technical resources can parameterize modules, constraints or mission events | A specific official dataset/resource at the center of gameplay; provenance; transformation pipeline; fallback snapshot |
| Best Use of Technology | Medium-high | Validated rules engine, accessible 2.5D game UI, optional constrained AI narration and deterministic fallback | Working integration, meaningful technical novelty, failure handling and no AI theater |
| Galactic Impact | Medium | Makes mission systems thinking accessible to learners with little space knowledge | A defined audience, learning outcome, user-test evidence and credible path to classroom/outreach use |
| Best Mission Concept | Very high | A complete near-future ISAM-enabled workshop and return-mission concept with explicit constraints | Plausible architecture, transparent fiction boundary, one coherent mission, source-backed trade-offs |
| Most Inspirational | High | Original Egyptian scientist, RIVO, comic wrong-orbit premise and empowering beginner role | Emotional polish, memorable demo, inclusive language and evidence that players want to retry or learn more |
| Best Storytelling | Very high | Story and gameplay express the same causal lesson; choices change Nadir's route home | Finished beginning/middle/end, character consistency, gameplay consequences inside the story, not a separate cutscene |
| Global Connection | Low-medium | Same-device co-op and future multilingual challenge sharing could connect learners | Actual multilingual or cross-location use; do not claim this from a language toggle alone |
| Art & Technology | Very high | Original identity, character acting, diegetic workshop interactions, motion/audio and a scientific engine | Final production assets, responsive implementation, interaction polish and provenance for every asset |
| Local Impact | Medium potential | Arabic support and testing with Egyptian learners could improve local access to space-systems education | Arabic localization, local user interviews/tests and a specific local learning need; not only that the team is Egyptian |

## Recommended award focus

### Primary: Best Mission Concept

Winning argument:

> A plausible, clearly bounded near-future mission concept turns integrated systems engineering into a short, replayable learning experience.

Required proof:

- workshop origin and transfer scenario are explicitly fictional but NASA-inspired;
- mission objective, vehicle, resources, constraints, operations and result form one coherent system;
- player choices have deterministic, sourced consequences;
- simplified rules and limitations are visible.

### Primary: Art & Technology

Winning argument:

> Original characters, motion, audio and a diegetic orbital workshop make a validated rule engine feel like a real game rather than a dashboard.

Required proof:

- approved identity used consistently;
- no generic AI dashboard aesthetic;
- every interaction has visual, numeric and audio feedback;
- accessible controls and captions;
- art and technical layers reinforce the same causal model.

### Supporting: Best Storytelling

The story must be playable:

- setup: build or inspect the workshop;
- disruption: wrong transfer;
- goal: get home;
- conflict: shared constraints;
- action: player decisions;
- result: causal mission review;
- change: Nadir learns to validate the whole system.

### Supporting: Best Use of Science

Science strength comes from methodology, not the number of formulas:

- source register;
- documented assumptions;
- deterministic rule table;
- unit and bounds validation;
- rule tests;
- visible evidence drawer;
- honest simplification labels.

## Categories not to force

- Do not add online multiplayer solely for Global Connection.
- Do not add AI solely for Best Use of Technology.
- Do not add many datasets solely for Best Use of Data.
- Do not claim Local Impact without local testing.
- Do not claim Galactic Impact from broad educational language alone.

## Pitch evidence checklist

- One sentence problem.
- One target learner.
- One measurable learning outcome.
- One authoritative NASA source shown live.
- One decision with a visible multi-system consequence.
- One failure and one successful retry.
- One source/simplification/result classification.
- One short user-test result.
- One sentence naming the project's limitations.
